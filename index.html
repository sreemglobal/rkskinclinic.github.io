Hair fall, explained

What can contribute to hair fall?

Hair shedding has more than one possible cause. The pattern, duration and other symptoms can help a clinician decide what needs to be assessed.

◌

Hereditary factors

Family history can play a role in some types of gradual hair thinning.

↗

Stress or recent changes

Illness, major stress or other changes in the body may be associated with increased shedding in some people.

＋

Health and nutrition

Some medical conditions, nutritional deficiencies and medicines can affect hair growth or shedding.

Remember: Some daily shedding is normal. Persistent, sudden or patchy hair loss may need medical assessment. Avoid starting supplements or medicines without appropriate advice.
When to seek advice

Notice a change in your hair or scalp?

Consider speaking with a qualified clinician if you notice:

  • Hair shedding that continues or concerns you.
  • Gradual thinning or a widening hair parting.
  • New bald patches or sudden hair loss.
  • Hair loss accompanied by scalp discomfort, redness or scaling.

Sudden or patchy hair loss should be assessed promptly. This page cannot determine the cause of an individual's symptoms.

Your consultation

What happens during a hair fall consultation?

The consultation is a chance to discuss your concern and understand what next steps may be appropriate.

Diagnosing

Discuss your hair concerns with the dermatologist and receive an assessment of your hair and scalp. Further investigations may be considered when clinically appropriate.

Treatment planning

The next steps focus on understanding possible underlying causes and discussing options suited to the clinical assessment—not a one-size-fits-all plan.

After-treatment guidance

When treatment is recommended, follow-up may include monitoring progress and advice on suitable hair care or diet where appropriate.

The exact assessment and any investigations vary by individual. A consultation does not guarantee a particular diagnosis or outcome.

Common questions

Hair fall consultation FAQs

Simple answers to help you understand the consultation process.

Is some hair shedding normal?

Yes. Hair naturally sheds as part of its growth cycle. The amount and pattern can vary. If shedding is persistent, sudden, patchy or concerning, seek an appropriate medical assessment.

What can cause hair fall?

Hair fall may be associated with several factors, including hereditary patterns, illness or stress, nutritional issues, certain medicines, scalp conditions and hair-care practices. The cause cannot be confirmed from a webpage alone.

Can frequent salon treatments or styling affect hair?

Frequent chemical processing, heat styling or tight hairstyles may contribute to hair-shaft damage or breakage. Ask the dermatologist about your specific hair-care routine and concerns.

Will the same hair fall plan work for everyone?

No. Hair and scalp concerns differ from person to person. The dermatologist will discuss next steps based on your history and assessment.

Will the dermatologist guide me after treatment?

If treatment is recommended, follow-up may be used to review progress and discuss suitable hair-care practices or other advice relevant to your situation.

Can the cause of hair fall be the same for everyone?

No. Hair loss can have different causes. A clinician considers the history, pattern and examination before discussing possible explanations.

Will I need blood tests?

Not everyone needs tests. A clinician may suggest investigations when they are relevant to the history and examination.

Will treatment be recommended at the first visit?

The next steps depend on the consultation findings. The clinician can discuss whether further assessment, investigations, treatment or follow-up may be appropriate.

Can I start oil, supplements or medicine before the consultation?

A product that is useful for one cause may not address another. Avoid starting medicines or high-dose supplements without appropriate advice, especially if you have other health conditions or take other medicines.
